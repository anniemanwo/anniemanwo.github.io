Confidential / Proposed editorial plan

One useful idea.
Connected channels.

Campaign theme: helping growing teams evaluate a shared digital workspace. The positioning centers on clarity, coordination, and ease of use; product claims would be checked with the product team before publication.

01 / LinkedIn · Weekly
Open the conversation.

Lead with a practical question: “Where does work lose momentum between teams?” Develop it into a carousel, a short point of view, and a discussion post.

02 / Blog · Twice monthly
Answer the next question.

Article direction: “How to evaluate a shared workspace for a growing team.” Cover user needs, access, onboarding, and a decision checklist. Link relevant social posts to the guide.

03 / Newsletter · Twice monthly
Give readers a reason to return.

Subject: “A clearer handoff starts with these questions.” Share one practical tip, a short insight from the guide, and one link to continue reading. Invite replies from subscribers.

Editorial consistency, engagement, and demand generation

Keep one approved message, visual palette, and tone across channels while adapting length and format. Maintain a calendar with audience, topic, owner, review status, and call to action. Review technical statements with a subject-matter expert.

Move from useful social content to a relevant guide, then to an optional newsletter subscription or product conversation. Track qualified visits, voluntary sign-ups, and inquiries alongside comments and saves; compare each channel by its role in the audience journey.

English / Product introduction

Give every team a clearer starting point.

When tools and updates live in different places, finding the next step can feel harder than the work itself. A shared workspace can bring the information people need into one place. Start by mapping the tasks your team repeats most often.

SEO / Content fundamentals

Useful content.
Easier to discover.

My focus is foundational on-page SEO: understanding what people search for, organizing a useful answer, and making each page’s purpose clear. For this proposed strategy, I would extend relevant social topics into helpful website content.

01 / Search intent
Start with a question.

Identify audience questions and relevant search terms, review the intent behind them, and build a focused content brief around one clear need.

02 / On-page content
Make the answer clear.

Write descriptive page titles and meta descriptions, use a logical heading structure, add useful internal links, and describe meaningful images with alt text.

03 / Review & refine
Learn from discovery.

With analytics access, review organic search impressions, clicks, and relevant visits. Refine titles and content around the questions that bring engaged readers.

Applied in this portfolio: A descriptive page title and meta description, structured headings, descriptive image alt text, and links to relevant sections. This demonstrates on-page fundamentals; search ranking and traffic results have not been measured.

From planning to learning

The first 90 days.

01 / Days 1–30
Listen & establish

Audit content, define audience questions, agree on voice, and build the first editorial calendar. Establish an analytics baseline.

02 / Days 31–60
Publish & learn

Test opening lines and formats. Review saves, replies, and engagement per impression; keep a record of what resonates.

03 / Days 61–90
Refine & repeat

Develop the strongest topics into repeatable series. Compare qualified website visits and inquiries with the baseline.

How I would measure and improve the content

Awareness: Monthly impressions and reach. Relevance: Saves, shares, replies, and engagement rate (interactions ÷ impressions × 100). Action: Tagged link clicks, qualified visits, and inquiries.

Review weekly, compare by format and topic, and change one creative variable at a time. Use monthly findings to adjust the next editorial calendar rather than treating follower growth as the only goal.
